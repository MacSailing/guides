What this course is

Anyone who transmits on a marine VHF radio in Canada needs an ROC(M). Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) issues it. Most recreational boaters earn it through a course like this one and a short exam with an accredited examiner. Learning the DSC (Digital Selective Calling) material earns the DSC endorsement, which you need to use the digital features of a modern radio.

The course covers three things. First, the rules that come with the radio. Second, how to talk on it so you're understood on a busy channel. Third, what to do when things go wrong: the Distress, Urgency and Safety calls that could save your boat or someone else's.

Warm-up

The phonetic alphabet

Radio audio is noisy, and "B", "D", "E" and "P" all sound alike through static. So we spell with words. Use these whenever you spell your boat name, a call sign or a place.

Numbers get a radio pronunciation too, so they don't blur together:

0Zero1Wun2Too3Tree4Fow-er 5Fife6Six7Sev-en8Ait9Nin-er

Practice

Type the word for each letter, then press Check. Spelling isn't strict: "Alpha" and "Alfa", "Juliet" and "Juliett", "Xray" and "X-ray" all count.
